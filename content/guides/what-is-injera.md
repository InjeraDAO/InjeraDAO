# What is injera?

**Injera is a soft, tangy, fermented flatbread with a distinctive spongy surface, central to many Ethiopian and Eritrean meals.** It often serves as both the base of a shared meal and a way to pick up stews, vegetables and other dishes.

## What is injera made from?

Traditional injera is closely associated with **teff**, a small grain long cultivated in the Horn of Africa. Recipes vary by region, household and restaurant. Some versions use teff alone; others blend teff with grains such as wheat, barley, sorghum or other locally available cereals. Do not assume a restaurant's injera is gluten-free without asking about its ingredients and preparation.

## Why does it have bubbles?

The batter is fermented before cooking. When it cooks on a wide, hot surface, tiny holes form across the top. The texture helps absorb flavorful sauces.

## What does injera taste like?

Many people describe injera as gently sour or tangy, with a soft, flexible texture. Flavor and acidity depend on grains, fermentation and preparation.

## How do you eat injera?

A large piece of injera is often laid under an assortment of stews and vegetables. Diners tear off a smaller piece and use it to pick up food. Sharing from a platter is common, although individual plates are also normal. Follow the host's preferences and local etiquette; washing hands before eating is always a good idea.

## Is injera Ethiopian or Eritrean?

Injera is deeply important in **both Ethiopian and Eritrean cuisines**, with shared histories and varied local practices. It is best to recognize both communities rather than claiming a single modern national ownership of every injera tradition.

## Where can I find injera?

Look for Ethiopian and Eritrean restaurants, specialist grocers, catering services and community food events. [injera.network](https://injera.network) is building a way to discover injera-serving places and connect with people who appreciate the food.

## What can I eat with injera?

Common pairings include shiro, lentil preparations, collard greens and other leafy vegetables, and meat-based stews such as doro wat. Dish names, ingredients, spices and preparation can differ significantly between communities and kitchens.

## What is the difference between injera and ordinary flatbread?

Injera is typically fermented and has a porous, springy texture. It is often large enough to line a communal platter and doubles as an edible utensil. Not all flatbreads use the same grains or cooking process.
